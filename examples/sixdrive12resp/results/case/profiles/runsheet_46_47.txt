RUNS 46 and 47 -- match_trace on the ESCAPE-CALIBRATED nonlinear plant
================================================================================
Third attempt at the same experiment.  Runs 42 (single-mode article) and 44
(all-modes article) both failed, for two different reasons, and both failures
are worth keeping because they define why this article is built the way it is.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY 42 AND 44 FAILED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RUN 42, single-mode article, strength 1.  Stopped after system ID.  Computed
from the system model and run 40's actual commanded drive, with the
voltage-to-force scaling verified (B = [[0],[M^-1 phi^T]], transformation =
identity, so 1 V = 1 N exactly): q_rms/q0_ref = 0.0067.  Only a tiny fraction
of a broadband 13.5 V drive lands on one mode, and shift goes as amplitude
squared, so even strength 400 gave -0.018%.  Not a moving plant.

RUN 44, all-modes article, strength 100.  The time integration overflowed:

    sdynpy_nonlinear_system_virtual_hardware.py:151:
        RuntimeWarning: overflow encountered in power
        return k3s * q ** 3 + c2s * qd * np.abs(qd)

with k3 reaching -2.55e19, NaNs then propagating into the FFT convolution.
That is ESCAPE, not a jump.  A softening cubic has a FINITE potential well:
the restoring force wn^2*q + k3*q^3 turns negative at q_esc = wn/sqrt(|k3|),
and beyond that the solution runs away.  The strength had been chosen against
the Duffing JUMP criterion (shift/zeta approaching 1), which never binds here
-- escape binds first and by a wide margin.

THE TRAP, and it is worth stating because it cost a run: you cannot buy
frequency shift by turning up k3.

    shift  ~  (3/8)|k3| A^2 / wn^2     rises linearly with |k3|
    q_esc  =  wn / sqrt(|k3|)          falls as 1/sqrt(|k3|)

so the shift available AT escape is 3/8 = 37.5%, fixed, whatever |k3| is.
Turning up strength raises the shift and moves the cliff toward you at the
same rate.  With random-drive peaks at ~4 sigma you need q_esc > 4*q_rms,
capping the usable shift near 2.3%, and with real margin nearer 0.5-1%.

Measured on the all-modes file, min over modes of q_esc/(4 q_rms):

    strength  1: 4.06 safe    4: 2.03 marginal   10: 1.28 marginal
             25: 0.81 ESCAPES               100: 0.41 ESCAPES

So strength 25 -- the value recommended before 100 -- would also have failed.
The binding mode was #31 at 837 Hz, where the randomised k3 draw landed at
2.7e18, and that mode is not the one carrying the shape change.  A single
uniform multiplier cannot serve both.  That is the defect this article fixes.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ARTICLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
sdynpy_frame6x12_system_nonlinear_escapecal.npz, built 2026-09-23 by
code/build_nonlinear_frf_system_escapecal.py.

Each mode's k3 is set from its own escape amplitude relative to the WORST-CASE
modal amplitude that mode sees under EITHER drive condition:

    |k3_i| = ( wn_i / (MARGIN * q_worst_i) )^2 ,   MARGIN = 10
    q_worst_i = max( q under run 40's control drive ,
                     q under a flat 1 V rms system-ID drive )

both computed by projecting the drive CPSD onto mode i through the physical
model -- measured, not assumed from a nominal voltage.  The result:

    33 flexible modes, 120.0 - 899.5 Hz
    first-order shift at 0 dB     0.268 - 0.375 %, median 0.375 %
    q_esc/(4 q) margin            2.500 on EVERY mode, under EITHER drive
    damping ratio at q_worst      x1.10 on every mode

THIS FILE WAS CALIBRATED THREE TIMES.  Both earlier attempts failed, in
opposite directions, and the record matters because the margin is now the only
thing standing between this and another lost run.

  Attempt 1 -- per-mode reference, MARGIN 6, referenced to the CONTROL drive
  alone.  Run 46's system ID overflowed at strength 1.  A flat system-ID drive
  and the shaped control drive excite the modes completely differently
  (per-mode ratios 0.25 to 1.2), so calibrating on one does not bound the
  other; the tightest mode was left with 1.267x margin on 4-sigma peaks, and
  the integration starts from rest so the switch-on transient ate it.
  Console kept as run46_attempt1_overflow_console.log.

  Attempt 2 -- a single global amplitude scale.  Safe, and useless: it puts
  essentially the whole shift on the one strongest mode, so the median LINE
  barely moves.  d_shape fell to 0.0025, below the 0.0070 noise floor.

  Attempt 3, this one -- per-mode again, but referenced to the worst case over
  both drives, MARGIN raised to 10.  Every mode keeps its shift AND every mode
  has q_esc >= 10*q under either drive, so 4-sigma peaks sit at 2.5x headroom
  by construction rather than by luck.

NOTE ON |k3|.  It reaches 1.15e20, LARGER than the value that overflowed in
attempt 1.  Not a contradiction: the overflow is a symptom of escape, not of
large k3.  k3*q^3 only overflows once q has already run away, and q cannot run
away while the margin holds.  But it does mean the margin is the whole
defence -- see the startup check.

The first-order formula overestimates -- checked against the single-mode
file's own numerical calibration it reads 8.9% where that builder solved
-4.0%, a factor of 2.2 -- so expect a realised shift nearer 0.5%.  Still 35x
what the all-modes file could deliver at a safe strength.

STRENGTH 1 IS THE DESIGN POINT AND THERE IS NO HEADROOM ABOVE IT.  Strength s
scales |k3| by s and q_esc by 1/sqrt(s):

    strength 1.00 -> margin 2.500      strength 2.00 -> margin 1.768
    strength 4.00 -> margin 1.250      strength 6.25 -> margin 1.000 (the cliff)

Margin 1.25 is essentially the 1.267 that failed in attempt 1, so strength 4
is already inside the region that overflowed.  USE 1.  If it misbehaves, drop
to 0.5 (margin 3.54).  Do NOT go above 2.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRE-REGISTERED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. A STEP IN THE DRIFT DIAGNOSTIC AT THE LEVEL CHANGE, median ~0.098 and 95th
   ~0.333 against a 0.0070 noise floor.  Quantitative, from the system model.
   Run 39 on the linear plant showed NO level-change artifact -- largest
   post-settling jump 0.0123 against neighbours 0.0121 and 0.0112 -- so any
   step here is the nonlinearity and nothing else.
2. NO OVERFLOW.  No "overflow encountered in power" in either console.
   Attempt 1 produced two within the system ID.  If it appears again at 2.5x
   margin the problem is NOT the calibration -- look at the integrator's
   timestep before recalibrating a fourth time.
3. THE LEVEL LOOP MUST CONVERGE, level dB within -0.5 to +0.5.  A level-only
   loop chasing a softening plant is the textbook failure mode.  If the level
   walks, that is a finding about the law, not a setup fault -- record it.
4. RUN 47's REFRESH COUNT SHOULD NOT DECAY TO NEAR ZERO the way run 40's did
   on the linear plant (901 -> 6-18).  Here the plant keeps moving with level,
   so the gate should keep firing.
5. NO ACCURACY PREDICTION.  Nothing is known about this law on a moving plant,
   and my predictions about match_trace's drive have been wrong twice.  It is
   entirely possible the refresh makes things WORSE, since control-level FRF
   estimates carry more distortion than the system ID does.  That would be a
   finding, and it is the reason to take 46 and 47 as a pair.

WHAT THESE RUNS DO NOT DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
They do not test gap-based rank (rcond negative).  That is run 48, against 47
as its control, and the distortion finding from run 44's system ID makes it
more pointed than it would have been on the linear plant.

They do not test the response-error trim or the optimal-diagonal laws here.
The trim in particular has a far stronger case on this article than on the
linear one, where the model mismatch measured 0.060 dB rms.

Runs 42 and 44 kept their system IDs.  They cost nothing to retain and they
are the only record of those two articles' identification.
