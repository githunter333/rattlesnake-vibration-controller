RUNS 35-38 -- the response-error trim on both optimal-diagonal laws
================================================================================
The trim was written 2026-09-21 and has NEVER run on hardware.  Four offline
tests pass, but offline the plant bias was uniform, constant and exactly known.
On the rig it is none of those.

Four runs, two matched pairs -- each law with the trim off and then on, back to
back in the same session on the same plant.  The off runs are not padding: the
code change gave both laws a set_test_level_db method where they had none, and
Rattlesnake gates on hasattr(control_function,'set_test_level_db'), so a code
path that never executed before now executes on every level change.  Offline
testing cannot reach that, because it never instantiates the framework.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RUNS 36 and 38 -- trim ON.  The actual test.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTIONS.  Written before the runs.  Each is judged against
that law's OWN off run (36 vs 35, 38 vs 37), never against run 32 or run 34,
which were taken on the pre-trim code.

  1. LEVEL -- the primary outcome.  Rattlesnake's Response Error panel overall
     should come closer to 0 dB than the paired off run by at least 0.3 dB.
     This is the one thing the trim exists to do.
  2. TRIM MAGNITUDE.  console trim_db median settling inside +/-1.5 dB.  A
     median parked at the +/-3.0 dB clamp means either the model bias here
     exceeds the trim's authority or it is integrating something it should not.
     Do NOT raise the clamp before knowing which.
  3. STABILITY.  drive_trace within 25% of the paired off run.  A 0.5 dB
     per-cycle step and 3 dB total authority cannot move the drive far; a large
     move means the trim is fighting the FRF-update loop.
  4. NO WIND-UP.  "at the +/-3 dB clamp" in the console should stay a small
     minority of in-band channel-bins.  Offline, at provably unreachable
     channel-bins the trim held at exactly 0.0000 dB -- but that was an exact
     algebraic construction, and here the solver-agreement gate has to do the
     same job against measurement noise.  THIS IS THE PREDICTION MOST LIKELY
     TO FAIL.
  5. LEVEL CHANGE.  exactly one "trim frozen" line per level change, and the
     trim resuming afterwards rather than sitting dead.
  6. GATE COVERAGE.  the console reports "N passed the solver-agreement gate"
     out of the scored total.  Offline that was 596-710 of 960.  If on the rig
     it collapses to a small fraction, error_threshold_db (1.0 dB) is too tight
     a gate on live data and the trim will barely act -- a null result caused
     by the gate, not by the plant, and it must not be read as "no bias".

EXPECT 38 TO CORRECT SHAPE MORE SLOWLY THAN 36, and this is predicted, not a
fault.  The overall level part of the correction is applied straight to the
stored drive CPSD and is immediate in both.  The per-channel shape part rides
the scheduler's step 3, and profile_03's frf_update_threshold of 0.05 has the
drift step claiming its full half-budget (10 of 20 bins) every call, leaving
step 3 much less room than in run 36 at threshold 0.5.  If 38's level improves
like 36's but its per-channel spread does not, that is the budget, not the trim.

WHAT WOULD BE A PROBLEM, in order of seriousness:

  a. Drive climbing monotonically.  Two integrators -- the trim, and the FRF
     update feeding the solve -- can chase each other.  Nothing in the design
     prevents that; the clamps only bound how fast and how far.
     ABORT if drive exceeds the paired off run by more than 50%.
  b. trim_db median swinging sign cycle to cycle: gain too high for this
     measurement noise.  Retry at gain 0.2 (field 11).
  c. Gate coverage collapsing -- see prediction 6.
  d. The trim correcting while the Response Error panel does not improve.  That
     would mean the measured diagonal the law is fed and the one the panel
     shows are not the same quantity, and the premise needs re-checking.

CAPTURE THE CONSOLE.  The trim prints one line for each of its first five
updates and then one in twenty.  The gate counts on those lines are the only
record of how much of the band the trim was allowed to touch, and none of it is
recoverable from the saved nc4 files afterwards.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THESE RUNS DO NOT DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
They do not test the trim against a KNOWN bias.  On this rig the true model
error is whatever it is; if it is small, 36 and 38 will show small corrections
and prove only that the trim does no harm.  Worth having, but it is not a
measurement of the loop's gain or bandwidth.  The honest next step -- not
attempted here -- is a deliberate-mismatch run: control against a system ID
taken at a different test level, so the bias is real and in a known direction.

They do not test octave_band_switching_control, which also inherits the trim and
has still never been run at all.

They do not touch the plant question.  All 38 runs remain on
sdynpy_frame6x12_system.npz, the low-damping plant.
