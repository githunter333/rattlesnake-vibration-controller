RUNS 51-54 -- the OPTIMAL DIAGONAL laws on the bounded-softening plant,
              and the first real test of the response-error trim
================================================================================
Built 2026-09-24, after runs 49 and 50 established the plant and the pair.

WHY THIS IS THE RIGHT NEXT EXPERIMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
optimal_diagonal is CLOSED on the identified plant model and OPEN on the
response error.  It re-solves against the live FRF every cycle (so it tracks a
moving plant) but its objective is the PREDICTED response, diag(H X H^H) -- it
never reads what the rig actually measured.  The response-error trim exists to
close exactly that gap.

On the LINEAR plant the trim had nothing to do.  Run 35 measured the
model-vs-measurement gap at 0.060 dB rms, which is why the trim was built with a
0.5 dB deadband and why runs 36-38 were allocated and never taken: there was no
error worth trimming.

On THIS plant there is.  Run 49 measured coherence falling from 0.996 to 0.938
at 0 dB and 0.684 at +4 dB, and the stream showed why -- the plant generates
harmonic and intermodulation content, out-of-band energy rising from -32.9 to
-16.5 dB.  Response power uncorrelated with the drive is not in diag(H X H^H),
so the model UNDER-PREDICTS the measured response by 10log10(1/gamma^2).  That
is a real, physical, one-sided error and it is precisely the trim's job.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRE-REGISTERED: THE ERROR THE TRIM WILL SEE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Computed from run 49's measured in-band coherence.  The trim acts only where
|error| exceeds its 0.5 dB deadband.

  level    median gap dB   frac >0.5 dB   frac >1 dB   frac >3 dB
   -18         0.018          0.004         0.000        0.000
   -12         0.018          0.004         0.000        0.000
    -6         0.030          0.006         0.000        0.000
     0         0.278          0.316         0.108        0.006
    +2         0.699          0.568         0.396        0.093
    +4         1.650          0.684         0.595        0.310
    +8         6.993          0.877         0.831        0.742
  (run 40, LINEAR plant at 0 dB, for scale: 0.018 dB, 0.005 above deadband)

READ THIS BEFORE CHOOSING THE LEVEL SCHEDULE.

  * Below -6 dB the trim WILL DO NOTHING.  The gap is 0.02-0.03 dB against a
    0.5 dB deadband; fewer than 1% of channel-bins qualify.  Expect trim-on and
    trim-off to be bit-comparable there, and treat that as a PASS -- it is the
    same null the verification suite checks (verify_response_trim.py: the trim
    is bit-identical when it does not fire).
  * At 0 dB the median gap is 0.278 dB, still BELOW the deadband.  Only 32% of
    channel-bins qualify, 11% exceed 1 dB.  The trim will act on the tail only.
    This is the interesting marginal case.
  * At +4 dB the median gap is 1.65 dB, 68% of bins above deadband and 31% above
    3 dB -- which is the trim's TOTAL AUTHORITY.  This is where the trim has real
    work, and also where it will start clamping.
  * +8 dB: the gap is 7 dB, more than twice the clamp.  The trim will saturate
    everywhere.  Run 49 reached +8 safely but coherence there was 0.200 and the
    FRF is not usable.  DO NOT plan on +8 for these runs.

SCHEDULE: -18 (null check), 0 (marginal), +4 (the real test).  -12 and -6 can
be skipped -- the table says they carry no information for this question.  If
time is short, -18 and +4 alone answer it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. AT -18 dB THE TRIM MUST NOT FIRE.  Trim-on and trim-off should score the
   same to within run-to-run scatter.  If they differ materially, the trim is
   acting on something other than a real response error -- stop and look.
2. AT 0 dB the trim fires on roughly a third of channel-bins and should produce
   a SMALL improvement, well under 1 dB.  Median gap 0.278 dB is below the
   deadband by design.
3. AT +4 dB the trim has 1.65 dB of median error to correct and 3 dB of
   authority.  This is where it should show clearly -- or fail clearly.
4. THE TRIM CORRECTS A ONE-SIDED ERROR.  Distortion only ADDS response power,
   so the measured response is always at or above the model.  The trim should
   move drive DOWN, monotonically, and clamp at -3 dB where the gap exceeds it.
   If it moves drive up anywhere, something is wrong with the sign or the gate.
5. NO ACCURACY PREDICTION vs match_trace.  These are different laws with
   different objectives; run 49/50 is not the baseline for these.  The baseline
   for each is its own trim-off partner.
6. SCORE ON THE COHERENT COLUMN.  Raw rms counts distortion as signal -- that is
   what wrecked run 49's Result 2, and at these levels the distortion is larger,
   not smaller.  Any claim made on raw rms here will be wrong in the same way.
