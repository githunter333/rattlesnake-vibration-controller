RUNS 35 and 36 -- the response-error trim, first time on the rig
================================================================================
The trim was written 2026-09-21 and has NEVER been run on hardware.  Four offline
tests pass, but offline the plant bias was uniform, constant and exactly known.
On the rig it is none of those.  These two runs are a control pair: the same
profile with the trim off and on, back to back, same session, same plant.

WHY IT EXISTS.  optimal_diagonal_control is closed on the identified plant model
-- it re-solves against the live FRF every cycle, which is what runs 29/31/32
measured -- and was open on the response error.  Error the FRF does not explain
(H1 bias, extraneous input, drive clipping, a nonlinearity that never appears in
a linear FRF) was invisible to the law and stood uncorrected, because nothing
compared the measured response with the specification.  The trim is the missing
integrator.

START AT -12 dB as usual, let it settle, then go to 0 dB.  The level change is
part of the test: the trim is supposed to stand itself down for three cycles
across it and then resume with its accumulated correction intact.

PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTIONS.  Written before the run.

  1. LEVEL.  Rattlesnake's Response Error panel overall should come CLOSER to
     0 dB than run 35, by at least 0.3 dB.  This is the one thing the trim is
     for and it is the primary outcome.
  2. TRIM MAGNITUDE.  console trim_db median should settle somewhere inside
     +/-1.5 dB.  A median sitting at the +/-3.0 dB clamp means either the model
     bias on this rig is larger than the trim's authority, or the trim is
     integrating something it should not be -- either way, do not raise the
     clamp before understanding which.
  3. STABILITY.  drive within 25% of run 35.  The trim has a 0.5 dB per-cycle
     step limit and only 3 dB of total authority, so it cannot move the drive
     far; a large move means it is fighting the FRF-update loop.
  4. NO WIND-UP.  "at the +/-3 dB clamp" in the console should stay a small
     minority of in-band channel-bins.  Offline, at provably unreachable
     channel-bins the trim held at exactly 0.0000 dB; on the rig the gate has to
     do that job against real noise instead of an exact construction.  This is
     the prediction most likely to fail.
  5. LEVEL CHANGE.  exactly one "test level ... trim frozen" line per level
     change, and the trim must resume afterwards rather than sitting dead.

WHAT WOULD BE A PROBLEM, in order of seriousness:

  a. Drive climbing monotonically.  Two integrators (the trim, and the FRF
     update feeding the solve) can chase each other.  Nothing in the design
     prevents this; the step and total clamps only bound how fast and how far.
     ABORT if drive exceeds run 35 by more than 50%.
  b. The trim oscillating -- trim_db median swinging sign cycle to cycle.  That
     is the gain too high for this measurement noise.  Retry at gain 0.2.
  c. Most in-band channel-bins failing the solver-agreement gate, so the trim
     never gets to act.  That would mean error_threshold_db (1.0 dB) is too
     tight a gate on live data, which offline testing could not have shown.
  d. The trim correcting and the Response Error panel NOT improving.  That would
     mean the measured diagonal the law is fed and the one the panel displays
     are not the same quantity, and the whole premise needs re-checking.

CAPTURE THE CONSOLE.  The trim prints one line per update for the first five
updates and then one in twenty; the gate counts on those lines are the only
record of how much of the band the trim was allowed to touch.

WHAT THESE RUNS DO NOT DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
They do not test the trim against a KNOWN bias.  On this rig the true model error
is whatever it is; if it happens to be small, run 36 will show a small correction
and prove only that the trim does no harm.  That is still worth having, but it is
not a measurement of the loop's gain or bandwidth.  A deliberate mismatch run --
control on a system ID taken at a different level, so the bias is real and in a
known direction -- would be the honest next step and is not attempted here.

They do not touch the plant question.  All 36 runs remain on
sdynpy_frame6x12_system.npz, the low-damping plant.
