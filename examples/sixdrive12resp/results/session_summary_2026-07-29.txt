1. STANDALONE ANALYTICAL COMPARISON (buzz vs optimal_diagonal_control)
===============================================================================
Built a reproducible offline pipeline (no Rattlesnake GUI needed) to compare
Rattlesnake's built-in buzz_control_class against the custom SDP-based
control_laws/optimal_diagonal_control.py:

  - compute_frf_frame6x12.py   (sdynpy env)  -> FRF H(f) for the 8x6 control set
  - build_flat_spec_large.py   (either env)  -> flat 0.001 g^2/Hz target spec
  - compare_buzz_vs_optimal_diagonal.py (rattlesnake env, needs cvxpy)

Buzz baseline = optimal_diagonal_control's own closed-form pinv solve (with
cross terms pulled from a clean, noise-free survey CPSD = H @ H^H).
Optimal result = same control law run to full convergence (n_deferred == 0).

Findings at original 1% damping:
  - Diagonal tracking error: buzz RMS 5.78 dB vs optimal-diagonal 5.64 dB
    overall -- only a modest difference in aggregate, but nodes 13/14 (farthest
    from the drives, dominated by a real structural antiresonance null) account
    for most of the error on BOTH methods and can't be improved by either.
  - Drive RMS: buzz ~64.8 (RSS across 6 drives) vs optimal-diagonal ~3.5 --
    optimal-diagonal used ~18x LESS drive energy for essentially the same
    tracking accuracy. Root cause: buzz's pinv-based synthesis applies H+ on
    both sides of a bilinear CPSD transform, so near-singular directions in H
    (e.g. a -29 dB antiresonance at 116 Hz, drive node 1 -> response node 8)
    get amplified quadratically. optimal_diagonal_control's SDP has a
    Tikhonov term (reg=1e-6 default) that directly penalizes this.

===============================================================================
4. REAL BUG FOUND AND FIXED: SDP-refinement scheduler starvation
===============================================================================
_refine_batch splits its per-call SDP budget (max_bins_per_update, default 20)
into two tiers: (1) re-solve already-refined bins whose FRF has drifted, then
(2) spend whatever's left on new, not-yet-refined worst-error bins. In the
live GUI, small run-to-run jitter in the measured FRF was enough to trip the
5% drift threshold on already-refined bins EVERY call, so tier 1 claimed the
ENTIRE budget every time, forever -- tier 2 got zero, and n_deferred froze at
~857/901 in-band bins indefinitely (confirmed over a 602-call, ~5 minute run
with drive levels and response error completely static the whole time).

Fix: capped tier 1 to at most half of max_bins_per_update, guaranteeing tier 2
always gets some budget regardless of how much (real or false-positive) drift
tier 1 sees. After the fix, a live run converged cleanly: n_deferred dropped
steadily call over call until reaching 0 at cum_sdp_refinements=901 (all
in-band bins), matching the analytical result almost exactly.

===============================================================================
5. CLOSED-LOOP FRF IDENTIFICATION BIAS ("Update Transfer Function During
   Control" = Y)
===============================================================================
With that setting ON, self_predicted_rms_db_per_channel ballooned to 30-45 dB
across every channel after ~17 minutes of continuous running, even though
n_deferred stayed at 0 (looked "converged"), while Rattlesnake's live Response
Error panel stayed a stable ~7-12 dB. H-drift diagnostics showed H was
continuously drifting away from its initial value for the entire run, never
settling -- a classic closed-loop identification bias: once the drive signal
is shaped by the control law to hit a flat target, it's no longer the
broadband/independent excitation the H1 estimator assumes, so re-estimating H
from that shaped signal degrades the estimate over time.

Fix (config, not code): turned "Update Transfer Function During Control" OFF.
H then stayed exactly static (since_last_solve and since_initial drift both
0.0000), and self-predicted error matched the clean analytical prediction
almost exactly: [4.24, 1.16, 1.98, 1.39, 0.86, 0.57, 10.84, 18.25] dB vs
analytical [4.34, 1.12, 1.93, 1.45, 0.91, 0.66, 11.18, 10.1] dB.

Remaining ~1-4 dB gap between self-predicted and Rattlesnake's live-measured
error (nodes 7-12) is attributed to ordinary system-ID estimation noise
(finite System ID Averages / Frames in CPSD), not a bug.

===============================================================================
6. DAMPING CHANGE: 1% -> 3-5%
===============================================================================
Original system (build_sdynpy_demo_frame6x12.py) used Rayleigh damping
(C = alpha*M + beta*K) tuned to zeta_target=0.01, giving modal damping ratios
0.70%-1.19% (mean 0.89%) across the 33 flexible modes, 120-900 Hz.

Per request, regenerated damping directly on the existing system file
(mass/stiffness/geometry/channels untouched) targeting zeta_target=0.04:
  - New alpha=51.68, beta=1.519e-5 (both simply 4x the original, since the
    Rayleigh solve is linear in zeta_target with the same anchor modes)
  - Resulting damping ratio range: 2.80%-4.75% (mean 3.57%)
  - Original (1%) system file backed up as sdynpy_frame6x12_system.npz.bak_1pct

At the higher, more realistic damping level, optimal-diagonal still wins
decisively on BOTH axes: noticeably lower tracking error on the well-coupled
channels (nodes 7-12) AND roughly 4-5x lower drive RMS than buzz. Higher
damping made the whole system easier to control for both methods (fewer
extreme narrowband peaks/nulls to fight), at the cost of needing more drive
energy overall to hit the same absolute target level (lower damping = lower
Q = higher average transmission gain = less drive needed, and vice versa) --
an expected physical trade-off, not an anomaly.
