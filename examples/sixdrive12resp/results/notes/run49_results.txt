RESULT 1 -- THE PLANT MOVED, AND ONLY WHERE IT WAS BUILT TO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Across a 12.7x span of drive voltage (-18 to +4 dB) the LINEAR CONTROL GROUP
went 0.0284 -> 0.0422.  That is flat; it is estimator noise.  Over the same span
the SOFTENED band went 0.0376 -> 0.6979, an 18x rise, and the ratio between them
climbed monotonically 1.32 -> 16.53.

No confound produces that pattern.  A drifting estimator, a level-loop artifact
or a distortion effect would move both bands; only a plant that softens above
300 Hz and nowhere else separates them monotonically with level.

The six linear modes were the best decision of the build.  They cost nothing --
they are the modes that were already there -- and they converted "the FRF
changed" into "the FRF changed in exactly the 27 modes we made nonlinear and in
none of the 6 we did not", inside a single run, with no second experiment.

RESULT 2 -- CONTROL QUALITY APPEARS TO IMPROVE WITH LEVEL.  IT DOES NOT.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
As scored, rms falls monotonically: 7.89 dB at -18 dB, 6.63 at +4, 4.96 at +8,
with %out 26.3 -> 19.6.  That is backwards -- the plant is moving AWAY from the
frozen FRF the drive was solved on -- and it was initially logged here as an
unexplained finding.  It is an ARTIFACT.  Resolved 2026-09-24 from the stream.

Score only the part of the response CORRELATED WITH THE DRIVE (multiply the
response auto-spectra by the measured coherence) and the improvement vanishes:

    level   rms_db as scored   rms_db coherent part   median coherence
     -18         7.89                 7.91                 0.9959
      -6         7.95                 7.99                 0.9930
       0         7.72                 8.10                 0.9380
      +2         7.21                 8.10                 0.8514
      +4         6.63                 8.29                 0.6839
      +8         4.96                 8.29                 0.1999

Control is FLAT to slightly WORSE with level, 7.9 -> 8.3 dB, which is what the
physics says it should be.  The apparent gain was distortion power, uncorrelated
with the drive, filling in the response spectrum and pulling the rms down.  The
scoring metric cannot tell driven response from distortion, and neither can the
law: match_trace is matching the trace of a response that is partly not its own.

At -18 dB this is a band-edge skirt: down 38 dB within 300 Hz of the edge, then
a floor.  That reproduces the -33 dB skirt-only result found earlier and is the
linear picture.  At +8 dB it is a broadband shelf still at -24.8 dB out at
Nyquist -- harmonic and intermodulation content generated by the plant, not
leakage.  A smaller difference-frequency rise appears below the band as well
(50-80 Hz, -48.5 -> -40.0 dB).

So the nonlinearity is not merely a level-dependent linear FRF.  It generates
energy at frequencies the drive never excited, and by +8 dB that energy is 2.2%
of the in-band power.

CONSEQUENCE FOR RUN 50, WHICH HAS REFRESH ON.  Refreshing replaces the FRF with
a control-time estimate.  Above 0 dB that estimate carries a third or more of
its response power uncorrelated with the drive, so the gate would be feeding the
solve an FRF that is progressively junk.  This is pre-registered item 6 -- "it
is entirely possible the refresh makes things WORSE" -- now with a mechanism and
a number.  RUN 50 SHOULD STOP AT 0 dB, +2 AT THE MOST.  Going higher measures
how the law behaves when handed a bad FRF, which is a legitimate experiment but
a different one, and should be run deliberately.

THE JUMP THAT DID NOT HAPPEN, AND WHY I KEPT PREDICTING IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Predicted first jump, revised four times as better drive data arrived:
    from the -6 dB drive   +4.5 dB
    from the  0 dB drive   +5.0 dB
    from the +2 dB drive   +6.0 dB
    from the +4 dB drive   +8.0 dB, 4 of 27 modes past the fold
MEASURED at +8 dB: loop gain 0.707, ZERO modes past the fold.

The mechanism I kept missing: match_trace re-solves the DRIVE every cycle
against the trace of the measured response.  Turning refresh off freezes the
FRF, not the drive.  As the plant softens and the response grows, the law backs
the drive off where it grew -- worth roughly 2 dB of effective level at +8 --
and that pushback suppresses the bifurcation.  Scaling a measured drive forward
in level assumes a drive that does not adapt, and this one does.

So the open caveat from the build ("the closed loop could move the gain either
way and I cannot predict which") is resolved, in the helpful direction: THE
CONTROLLER SUPPRESSES THE JUMP.  That is a genuinely useful thing to know about
this law on a softening plant, and it was only learnable by running it.

Prediction record on this article is now 5 wrong, 1 right.  The one that held
was 0 dB shift predicted from the -6 dB drive (0.699%/0.986%, gain 0.176 against
measured 0.745%/1.164%, gain 0.165 -- 7% on the median).  It held because it was
a one-step extrapolation from measured data; every failure was a multi-step
extrapolation through a mechanism that was not in the model.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE DOWN-SWEEP -- NOT A HYSTERESIS RESULT.  DO NOT CITE IT AS ONE.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
+4 dB on the way down differs from +4 dB on the way up: FRF shape difference
0.6827 in the softened band against 0.0450 in the linear band, a factor of 15.
It settled (c1_med flat at 0.62 over the last 8 cycles, against 0.52 on the way
up), so it is not simply mid-transient.

BUT the level loop landed at -0.80 dB coming down against +0.51 dB going up.
The two points are 1.31 dB apart in drive, so they are not the same operating
condition and the comparison is confounded.  Level difference alone predicts the
implied shift should fall 1.640% -> about 1.21%; measured 1.379%, i.e. about 14%
MORE softened than the level accounts for.  That is the direction hysteresis
would push, but 14% is inside the uncertainty of this estimate.

With loop gain 0.707 at +8 and no mode past the fold, there is no bistability in
the model, so there is no fold for a hysteresis loop to form around.  The honest
statement is: the two +4 dB points differ, most of the difference is the level
loop, and nothing here establishes path dependence.  It needs a repeat with the
level settled at both points before it means anything.
