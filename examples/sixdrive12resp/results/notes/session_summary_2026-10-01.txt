RESULT 1 -- COHERENCE ANSWERS A DIFFERENT QUESTION THAN THE ONE ASKED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Rattlesnake already computes multiple coherence per control channel and saves it
as frf_coherence.  The identity diag(H Sxx H^H)/diag(Syy) reproduces it to the
digit, so the coherent and incoherent response power split is available offline,
per channel and per line, from data already on disk.

But it cannot see the softening.  Under Gaussian excitation an amplitude-
dependent stiffness splits the response into a part exactly proportional to the
drive plus an orthogonal remainder, and coherence counts the first part as
linear.  That first part IS the whole 1% frequency shift and the whole 12.4%
damping rise measured on 09-29.  Coherence cannot tell a moved linear system
from the original one.  Run 51 at -18 dB reads 0.9973 on a plant that has
already softened.

So the two measurements are complementary halves and neither alone is the
nonlinearity:

    df/f and zeta/zeta0        the equivalent-linear half -- a different
                               linear plant
    1 - gamma^2                the residual half -- what looks like nothing
                               linear at all

What coherence does see works.  The third harmonic lands at 1250-1450 Hz, out of
band, so in-band incoherence is essentially all intermodulation.  On
channel 1 at 0 dB, 33.4% of in-band response power is not linearly
explained by the six drives, against 1.2% at -18 dB.

RESULT 2 -- THE SAVED CPSDs ARE REFERENCED TO FULL LEVEL, NOT TO THE TEST LEVEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The -18 dB response matches the full specification to -0.17 dB, and the saved
specification matrix is byte-identical at every level.  The saved response and
drive CPSDs are therefore referenced to the FULL-LEVEL specification, not
absolute at the test level.

This is why a -18 dB and a 0 dB trace sit nearly on top of each other instead of
18 dB apart.  Any plot or statement that treats them as absolute G^2/Hz at the
test level is wrong by the test level.  All figures from this session label the
axis accordingly.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULT 3 -- THE PUBLISHED COHERENCE NUMBERS ARE BIASED HIGH, AND BY HOW MUCH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
At one line, gamma^2 = ||P_X y||^2 / ||y||^2: the fraction of the response
vector's energy lying in the p-dimensional subspace the drives span.  A response
unrelated to the drives is isotropic in C^n_d, so a fixed p-dimensional subspace
catches p/n_d of it -- exactly, the ratio being Beta(p, n_d - p).  With six
drives and twenty frames that is 0.30.  A response with NO linear relationship
to the drives measures 0.30 here, not zero.  Monte Carlo at this configuration,
40000 trials:

    true gamma^2    0.00    0.40    0.70    0.90    0.99
    measured        0.3004  0.5747  0.7875  0.9296  0.9930
    corrected back  0.0006  0.3925  0.6964  0.8994  0.9900

The correction is gamma_adj^2 = 1 - (1 - gamma_hat^2) * n_d/(n_d - p).  Nothing
in it is fitted; it is a function of the estimate and two counts.

n_d WAS THE ASSUMPTION, AND IS NOW MEASURED.  frames_in_cpsd is 20 but the
frames overlap 50% with a Hann window.  Pushing noise uncorrelated with the
drives through the real Welch chain:

    no overlap      bias 0.2999    n_eff 20.01    factor 1.4283
    50% overlap     bias 0.3109    n_eff 19.30    factor 1.4513

The no-overlap case recovers the theory to 0.03%, which validates the chain.
The rig's overlap costs 0.7 of an average.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE 09-26 REPORT.  Every coherence figure in it is high by about
0.30*(1-gamma^2), and the coherent-column scoring used raw gamma^2 as a weight,
so the weighting was systematically generous exactly where distortion was worst.
"Coherence falls from 0.938 to 0.697" is really 0.911 to 0.567 -- the effect is
larger, not smaller, but the numbers as published are wrong.

A CONJUGATION TRAP, recorded because it cost a wrong answer.  Sxx must be X^H X,
conjugating the FIRST factor.  Conjugating the second gives conj(X^H X), the
quadratic form then uses the transpose of the inverse, and the null case reads
0.41 instead of 0.30.  Real-valued test data hides it completely -- an isolation
test on real frames returned 0.2998 and looked correct.  The first attempt at
the n_eff measurement was wrong for this reason and was nearly reported as
evidence that the correction itself was bad.  The production code in
spectral_processing.py uses the correct convention.

RESULT 4 -- THE CONTROL IS BETTER THAN THE ERROR NUMBER SAYS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Control channel 1 at 0 dB, in band, against a 0.9492 G specification:

    total response                  1.3195 G    +2.86 dB
    coherent, gamma^2 x total       1.0772 G    +1.10 dB
    coherent, bias-corrected        0.9524 G    +0.03 dB

The part of the response the six drives explain linearly, with the finite-
average inflation removed, is within three hundredths of a decibel of
specification.  The apparent 2.86 dB overshoot at full level is almost entirely
content that is not linearly related to the drive -- not a control error in any
sense the law could have fixed by commanding differently.

Two caveats before this is quoted.  It is one channel, and channel 1 is not one
of the two the rank-5 solver concedes, so it flatters the set; the other seven
need checking.  And the whole-band number depends on the bias correction, which
is now measured rather than assumed but is still a mean correction applied line
by line.

RESULT 5 -- A CONTROL FAILURE AT THE LINEAR / SOFTENED BAND BOUNDARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Channel 1 runs +15.03 dB over specification at 294 Hz at 0 dB, against +5.14 dB
at 306 Hz at -18 dB.  In the 200-400 Hz window four modes are exactly linear
(214.37, 246.94, 249.18, 286.25 Hz) and three soften (300.86, 306.47, 335.24 Hz,
coming down 0.97-0.99% at 0 dB).  The gap between the top linear mode and the
first softened one closes from 14.61 to 11.70 Hz, a 20% narrowing, and the
failure sits IN THAT GAP rather than on either peak.  The mode that does not
move is the one whose control fails, because the FRF shape between the two
changes even though neither peak is where the error is.

It is not a clean control miss.  Over 288-300 Hz at 0 dB the response is
+14.07 dB over specification, of which +9.84 dB is coherent and only +4.86 dB
survives the bias correction; median coherence there falls to 0.40 against 0.997
at -18 dB.  Both mechanisms fire on the same lines.  Across the whole 200-400 Hz
window the bias-corrected coherent response is +0.59 dB over specification, so
nearly all of the apparent +6.14 dB error in this band is content the drives do
not explain linearly.

This is very likely the same mechanism as the tail leakage into the linear
control group seen at +8 dB in run 49, but here it is visible at the design
point.  It wants confirming against a frozen-H0 calculation, which would
separate plant movement from distortion cleanly instead of by inference.

RESULT 6 -- BOTH LAWS CONCEDE THE SAME TWO CHANNELS, BEFORE THE PLANT MOVES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every moving-plant spectrum was rescored by one definition, validated against
the stored runs_51_52_55_optdiag.json values to three decimals.  Level error for
run 50 did not exist before and is computed here.

On accuracy optimal_diagonal is 1.9 dB better than match_trace at every level
and holds that as the plant moves.  On level it loses completely: +8.00 dB at
+4 dB demand against match_trace's +0.51 dB.  Neither of those is new, but the
per-channel breakdown is:

    channel            1     2     3     4     5     6     7     8
    optimal_diagonal  5.08  1.93  3.04  2.56  1.98  2.26 11.08  8.50
    match_trace       6.38  4.77  5.66  5.18  4.09  3.99 12.79 11.32

Both laws concede channels 7 and 8, which is what a rank-5 plant does to eight
control channels, and both concede them ALREADY AT -18 dB, before the plant has
moved at all.  The concession is a rank consequence, not a nonlinearity
consequence.  The entire difference between the laws is in the six they can
reach: 1.9-5.1 dB against 4.0-6.4 dB.

figure 13 legend was moved to "fix" an overlap, landed directly on top of both
match_trace traces, and was shipped without re-rendering to check -- the
