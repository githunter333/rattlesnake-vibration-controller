--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE FOUR RESULTS THAT MATTER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE DAMPING IS 88% MORE AMPLITUDE-DEPENDENT THAN INTENDED.  The builder
   sizes the quadratic damping term with the SINUSOIDAL describing function:

       c2[mask] = (ZETA_RATIO - 1.0) * zeta0[mask] * 3*np.pi / (4 * sig_t[mask])

   The article only ever runs under random excitation, where power balance gives
   c_eq = 2*sqrt(2/pi)*c2*sigma_v.  The two differ by (3/4)*sqrt(2pi) = 1.8800.
   ZETA_RATIO = 1.10 realises +0.124 at 0 dB, not +0.10.

   This is the same class of error as the stiffness calibration fixed in August
   -- a sinusoidal describing function applied to a Gaussian process -- and it
   survived because nothing had ever asked what the damping actually was.

   NOT A DEFECT IN ANY RUN.  The plant is bounded and stable at every level
   tested; every saved run stands.  What is wrong is the documented intent.
   Rescaling c2 would make the name true but changes the plant and orphans the
   existing run set; renaming the constant does not.  Renaming is the cheaper
   repair and neither is urgent.

2. PART OF THE DAMPING RISE IS FREE.  The modal equation is
   qdd + 2*zeta0*wn*qd + g*wn^2*q = f.  Softening scales the stiffness and
   leaves the damping COEFFICIENT alone, so the frequency falls to wn*sqrt(g)
   while c does not move and the damping RATIO rises as 1/sqrt(g) with no
   quadratic term at all.  That is 1.0100 of the 1.1239 at 0 dB -- about 8% --
   and 1.0988 of 1.6215 at +8 dB.

   A softening-only plant is still not a constant-damping plant.

3. ABOVE 0 dB THE UNIFORMITY BREAKS, ONE MODE AT A TIME.  At 0 dB every
   softened mode sits between 0.901% and 0.999%: the calibration is uniform to
   a tenth of a percent, which is what it was designed to be.  Above 0 dB the
   median stays orderly while single modes break away --

       +2 dB   median 1.625%   464.4 Hz at  9.291%
       +4 dB   median 2.763%   306.5 Hz at 13.867%, 478.7 Hz at 12.54%
       +8 dB   median 8.995%   519.8 Hz at 30.676%, 499.6 Hz at 26.19%

   -- by the mechanism the loop-gain guard was written for: the shaped drive is
   notched at each resonance, the resonance slides off its own notch into a
   hotter part of the drive, and that mode's local loop gain passes 1 while the
   rest of the band is still well behaved.  The guard is evaluated at the 0 dB
   design point only, so it does not see this.

   CONSEQUENCE FOR THE RUN SET.  +4 dB and +8 dB are NOT "the same plant, driven
   harder".  They are a plant with a few modes far past the design point and the
   rest near it.  Run 49's +8 dB point should be read that way, and this is the
   likely mechanism behind the tail leakage into the linear control group seen
   there -- a mode shifted 30% has skirts well below 300 Hz.

4. TWO DIFFERENT ANSWERS, AND BOTH ARE CORRECT.  Everything above is the
   EQUIVALENT-LINEAR answer: the best linear system matching the response in a
   mean-square sense.  That is the right number for anything consuming an FRF in
   an rms sense, which is every control law here.  A modal fit to a MEASURED FRF
   returns something else, because the resonance wanders with the instantaneous
   amplitude across the record.  Nine modes, direct integration:

     - the FRF PEAK moves LESS than the equivalent-linear frequency, by a factor
       0.836 on average (0.676 - 0.962).  Small excursions dominate by count and
       soften least, so the peak sits above the mean-square answer.
     - the HALF-POWER width reads HIGH, by roughly 1 + 0.12*(shift in %)^2.  A
       few percent at 0 dB; 30-60% by +4 dB with no damping mechanism having
       changed.

   Any damping number taken off a measured FRF on this article above about 1%
   shift is an upper bound, not a measurement.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VERIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
fs = 20 kHz, 24 realisations x 20 s = 480 s per mode, shaped run-40 drive at
0 dB.  Linear and nonlinear simulations share identical noise and integrator, so
the scheme's own frequency bias cancels in the ratio.

  mode        sigma_q sim/pred   c_eq meas/pred   E|qd|^3 / sigma_v^3
                                                  (Gaussian = 1.5958)
  300.9 Hz        1.0007            1.0003              1.5993
  464.4 Hz        1.1467            1.0917              2.2830
  728.2 Hz        1.0071            1.0027              1.6118
  899.5 Hz        1.0098            1.0039              1.6024

Three of four confirm the response to 1% and the equivalent damping coefficient
to 0.4%, with the measured third absolute moment landing on the Gaussian value
to three digits -- the assumption tested rather than asserted.

The 464.4 Hz mode is the exception and it is informative.  Its velocity is
markedly non-Gaussian (2.283), its response runs 15% over prediction and its
damping 9% over.  It is the same mode that breaks away first at +2 dB.  Heavy
tails and early runaway are one phenomenon seen two ways.

The frequency check is separated from the response check deliberately.  An H1
estimate formed from the shaped control drive is starved of excitation exactly
at the peak, because match_trace notches the drive there, so the measured peak
is biased by however deep that mode's notch is.  The frequency numbers come from
flat-band excitation with sigma_q measured from the simulation and the closed
form evaluated at that measured sigma_q.

LOOSE ENDS FOUND WHILE CHECKING FIGURE 6
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The x1.3 / x6.5 / x16.5 / x8.9 annotations are the ratio of the softened band's
per-line FRF shape change to the linear band's at that level.  Confirming that
turned up two things:

  - THE SCRIPT THAT COMPUTED THE SHAPE METRIC IS GONE.  It was scratch.  Only
    run49_level_curve.json and the one-line prose definition in
    run49_results.txt survive; the exact normalisation is not reproducible from
    the repo.  If that metric is going to be quoted it should be rewritten and
    committed.  (The scripts written today are in the repo for this reason.)

  - "FLAT ACROSS A 12.7x SPAN OF DRIVE VOLTAGE" IS LOOSE, in run49_results.txt
    and in the 09-26 report that inherited it.  The 12.7x is drive from -18 to
    +4 dB, but the linear-band numbers quoted, 0.0284 -> 0.0422, run from -12 to
    +4, because -18 is the zero reference by construction.  Over that range the
    drive span is 6.7x.  The claim survives -- 1.5x against 18.6x -- but the
    sentence mixes two ranges.  Both should be corrected together.
