RESULT 1 -- OPTIMAL DIAGONAL BEATS MATCH_TRACE DECISIVELY, AT LOW LEVEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
At 0 dB, all on the coherent column (raw counts distortion as signal -- see
run49_results.txt Result 2):

    law                       rms coherent   %out   drive V   coherence
    optimal_diagonal (51)         5.58       15.2     9.33     0.9530
    match_trace refresh ON (50)   7.47       20.3    14.93     0.6967
    match_trace refresh OFF (49)  8.10       26.1    12.17     0.9380

1.89 dB better than the best match_trace result on 38% less drive, and it holds
coherence at 0.953 where match_trace-with-refresh falls to 0.697.  The whole
chain follows from asking less of the plant: less drive, less distortion,
cleaner FRF, better control.

At -18 dB the margin is larger still: 5.61 dB rms and 11.1% out against
match_trace's 7.38/22.7 -- 1.77 dB better on 43% of the drive power.

CAVEAT, and it is not small.  The aggregate hides a 6+2 split.  Per channel at
0 dB: 5.47 2.49 3.46 3.16 2.39 3.33 | 10.34 7.71.  The SDP concentrates six
drives on six channels and concedes two.  match_trace's split is milder.
Whether that trade is acceptable depends on whether channels 7 and 8 matter.

RESULT 2 -- OPTIMAL DIAGONAL LOSES LEVEL CONTROL AS THE PLANT SOFTENS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Run 51, level error vs specification:

    -18 dB   +0.31        0 dB   +1.98        +4 dB   +8.00

At +4 dB the response is EIGHT dB above specification and the law is doing
nothing about it: drive rose only 0.9 dB (9.33 -> 10.33 V) for a 4 dB increase
in demand while the response overshot by 8.  That is the "OPEN on the response
error" property, not as a subtlety but as an 8 dB miss -- the law minimises the
PREDICTED diag(H X H^H) and never reads what the rig measured.

The +4 dB point is therefore NOT a valid control point and must not be quoted as
one.  Note what it does to the scoring: coherent rms reads 5.53 there, better
than 0 dB, because coherence weighting strips distortion but not level error and
rms-about-zero partly cancels on a uniformly hot response.  rms alone is blind
to this failure.  Always read level_err alongside it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULT 3 -- THE RESPONSE-ERROR TRIM DOES NOT EARN ITS PLACE ON THIS LAW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This was the point of the exercise.  The trim was built to close the
response-error loop the law leaves open, and Result 2 is exactly the failure it
should fix.  It does not.

RUN 55 (leak fix) vs RUN 51 (trim off), at 0 dB, where the model gap is 0.209 dB
median with 29% of channel-bins above the 0.5 dB deadband -- the one level where
the trim has real work:

    run              rms raw  coherent  %out   level dB  drive V  coherence
    run55 trim ON      5.37      5.68   16.9    +2.71     11.69   0.8956
    run51 trim OFF     5.49      5.58   15.2    +1.98      9.33   0.9530

WORSE ON EVERY MEASURE.  0.10 dB worse on the coherent column, 1.7 points more
lines out, 0.73 dB further off level, 25% more drive, and coherence down from
0.953 to 0.896.  The raw column flatters it (5.37 vs 5.49) for the run-49
reason: distortion counted as signal.

WHY, and this is the useful part.  Per channel at 0 dB:

    run55 trim ON   5.35  2.93  4.18  3.82  3.05  3.58  |  9.20  7.42
    run51 trim OFF  5.47  2.49  3.46  3.16  2.39  3.33  | 10.34  7.71

The trim IMPROVED the two conceded channels and DEGRADED the six good ones.  It
is pulling drive toward channels the SDP had deliberately given up on.  The
solver concedes 7 and 8 because serving them costs more on the other six; the
trim cannot see that trade and buys them back at full price.

So the trim is not merely mistuned here -- it is solving the wrong problem.  It
treats a RANK-DEFICIENCY TRADE-OFF (8 control channels, 6 drives, rank 5) as if
it were MODEL ERROR.  On a square, well-conditioned plant the distinction would
not arise.  On this one it is the dominant term.

That does not condemn the trim in general.  Its premise -- that the law is blind
to error the FRF cannot explain -- is correct, and the distortion on this plant
is real (third harmonic -53 dB at -18 rising to -29 dB at 0 dB).  But on a
rank-deficient plant the achievability shortfall swamps the model error, and the
solver-agreement gate cannot separate them well enough to act on.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULT 4 -- THE WINDUP, AND THREE FAILED FIXES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Run 52 was the trim's FIRST live run; runs 36-38 were allocated in a previous
session and never taken.  It failed the pre-registered null check at -18 dB,
where the true model gap is 0.012 dB against a 0.5 dB deadband:

    trim_db[median=-1.51, min=-3.00, max=+3.00] after 261 updates
    drive 11.47 V rms against trim-off's 8.84 -- 30% more for the same response

CAUSE: integrator windup behind a conditional gate.  The solver-agreement gate
is evaluated BEFORE the error term, and gated bins got step_db = 0 -- they kept
whatever correction they carried, permanently.  The gate blocked harmful
accumulation and corrective UNWINDING equally.

THREE ATTEMPTS, all wrong, each caught by a test rather than by reasoning:

  1. Leak every idle bin.  Made the steady state sit at the deadband EDGE
     rather than zero -- the trim unwound until the error grew back past 0.5 dB,
     a limit cycle.  TEST 2 settled at -0.83 dB against a real -2.00 dB bias.
  2. Leak on any single gate failure.  With max_bins_per_update = 20 against 901
     in-band bins, a bin is re-solved once per ~45 cycles and its cached
     prediction is stale until then, so the gate fails for reasons unrelated to
     reachability.  TEST 2 settled at -1.05 dB.
  3. Leak after 50 CONSECUTIVE gate failures (committed, d068c0ce).  Passes all
     six tests and PARTIALLY works live -- see below.
  4. Pay down the counter on a pass instead of resetting it, to catch
     intermittent passers.  TEST 6 then failed: a real 3 dB model error settled
     at -0.58 dB with 1.11 dB residual.  REVERTED.

THE DEEPER PROBLEM, which is why attempt 4 could never have worked: a bin
WAITING to be re-solved fails the gate ~98% of the time, and a bin the solver
genuinely CANNOT reach also fails ~98% of the time.  Gate-failure FREQUENCY
cannot distinguish them at any threshold.  The discriminator is whether a bin
passes the gate ON THE CYCLES IT IS ACTUALLY RE-SOLVED, which means the counter
must be keyed to re-solve events -- _refine_batch telling _update_response_trim
which bins it touched.  That is real plumbing between two parts of the law that
do not currently talk, and given Result 3 it is now a LOW PRIORITY: fixing the
windup perfectly would still leave a trim that pulls drive toward conceded
channels.

WHAT THE COMMITTED FIX ACHIEVED (run 55 vs run 52, at -18 dB):

    clamped channel-bins   1700 -> ~980 steady state
    trim median            -1.51 dB -> +0.00 dB
    drive                  11.47 V -> 10.72 V   (trim-off is 8.84)

A partial cure: about 28% of the excess drive recovered.  The residual ~980
pinned bins are level-INDEPENDENT -- 10.72/10.76/10.90/11.24 V at
-18/-12/-6/-3 dB while the model gap goes 0.012 -> 0.35 dB -- which confirms
they are leftover convergence artifacts rather than a response to the plant.

RESULT 5 -- THE THIRD HARMONIC IS THE BEST NONLINEARITY INDICATOR ON THIS RIG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Run 55, mean response PSD in 1250-1450 Hz relative to the in-band mean:

    -18 dB  -53.47      -12 dB  -51.88      -6 dB  -42.88
     -3 dB  -36.36        0 dB  -29.46

24 dB of range over 18 dB of level, ~2.2 dB per dB, consistent with the cubic
amplitude scaling of an odd nonlinearity.  1250-1450 Hz is 3x the most-softened
mode cluster (412.7, 413.6, 438.3, 451.0, 454.7, 464.4, 478.7 Hz).  The kernel
g(q) = alpha + (1-alpha)exp(-(q/qt)^2) is odd in q, so it generates odd
harmonics only -- there is no matching feature at 2x, which would fall in band
at 830-960 Hz.  The plant has NO modes above 899.5 Hz, so nothing up there is
resonant; it is all distortion.

CONTRAST with the in-band error in the same mode cluster, 300-600 Hz:

    -18 dB  4.90      -12 dB  4.88      -6 dB  4.89      -3 dB  4.89 dB rms

FLAT.  Completely insensitive to the plant moving.  The in-band error in that
band is set by the rank-5 geometry of an 8-channel 6-drive problem, not by the
nonlinearity.

CONCLUSION WORTH CARRYING: on this rig the nonlinearity appears in the
OUT-OF-BAND HARMONIC long before it appears in the in-band control error.  The
harmonic needs no reference run and no second experiment -- it is free to
measure from any saved spectrum.  It is the indicator to watch.
