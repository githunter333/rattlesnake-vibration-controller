33 flexible modes, 120.0 - 899.5 Hz.  27 above 300 Hz are softened; the six
below are exactly linear (nonlinear term identically zero).  zeta0 on the
softened modes is 0.00701 - 0.01187.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ANSWER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Equivalent-linear (Gaussian statistical linearization) properties of the 27
softened modes.  "shift" is how far the frequency falls below its linear value.

  level    ---- frequency shift % ----    ---- zeta / zeta0 ----    of which
   dB      median    min       max        median    max           1/sqrt(g)
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   -18      0.015    0.011     0.016      1.0140   1.0269          1.0002
   -12      0.061    0.044     0.064      1.0283   1.0540          1.0006
    -6      0.242    0.181     0.254      1.0579   1.1086          1.0024
    -3      0.488    0.380     0.502      1.0838   1.1545          1.0049
     0      0.995    0.901     0.999      1.1239   1.2207          1.0100
    +2      1.625    1.569     9.291      1.1660   1.7394          1.0165
    +4      2.763    2.507    13.867      1.2280   1.9671          1.0284
    +8      8.995    6.558    30.676      1.6215   3.1833          1.0988

The six linear modes are identically 0.000% and 1.0000 at every level.

At the 0 dB design point the plant is a 1% frequency softening and a 12%
damping rise.  Below -6 dB both are under a quarter of a percent and a
6% damping rise -- effectively a linear article.

The fixed point converges to a residual of 1e-14 at every level, so the
numbers above are the converged self-consistent state, not an iteration count.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULT 1 -- THE DAMPING IS 88% MORE AMPLITUDE-DEPENDENT THAN INTENDED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The builder sets ZETA_RATIO = 1.10 and sizes the quadratic damping term with

    c2[mask] = (ZETA_RATIO - 1.0) * zeta0[mask] * 3*np.pi / (4 * sig_t[mask])

That is the SINUSOIDAL describing function for c2*qd*|qd|, c_eq = (8/3pi)*c2*V.
Under a Gaussian process the equivalent coefficient comes from power balance:

    E[|qd|^3] = 2*sqrt(2/pi) * sigma_v^3        =>   c_eq = 2*sqrt(2/pi)*c2*sigma_v

The two differ by (3/4)*sqrt(2*pi) = 1.8800.  The article is used only under
random excitation, so the intended +0.10 is +0.188 before feedback.  Response
suppression from the extra damping pulls it back, and the converged 0 dB state
is +0.124 rather than the +0.10 designed.

This is the same class of error as the stiffness calibration fixed in August:
a sinusoidal describing function applied to a Gaussian process.

NOT A DEFECT IN ANY RUN.  The plant is bounded and stable at every level tested
and every saved run remains valid.  What is wrong is the DOCUMENTED intent, and
ZETA_RATIO no longer means what its name says.  Two options, neither urgent:
rescale c2 by 1/1.8800 to make 1.10 true, or rename the constant and record
that the realised value is +0.124 at 0 dB.  Rescaling changes the plant and
invalidates the existing run set against it; renaming does not.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULT 2 -- PART OF THE DAMPING RISE IS FREE, AND IS NOT THE c2 TERM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The modal equation is  qdd + 2*zeta0*wn*qd + g*wn^2*q = f.  Softening scales the
stiffness by g and leaves the damping COEFFICIENT alone, so the natural
frequency falls to wn*sqrt(g) while c does not move, and the damping RATIO rises
as 1/sqrt(g) with no quadratic term at all.

At 0 dB that accounts for 1.0100 of the 1.1239, so about 8% of the damping rise
is a bookkeeping consequence of the frequency shift and 92% is the c2 term.  At
+8 dB the free part is 1.0988 of 1.6215.

Worth stating because it means a softening-only plant -- one with c2 = 0 -- is
still not a constant-damping plant.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULT 3 -- ABOVE 0 dB THE UNIFORMITY BREAKS, ONE MODE AT A TIME
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
At 0 dB every softened mode sits between 0.901% and 0.999% -- the calibration is
uniform to within a tenth of a percent, which is what it was designed to be.

Above 0 dB the median stays orderly while single modes break away:

   +2 dB   median 1.625%   one mode at 9.291%    (464.4 Hz)
   +4 dB   median 2.763%   306.5 Hz at 13.867%, 478.7 Hz at 12.54%
   +8 dB   median 8.995%   519.8 Hz at 30.676%, 499.6 Hz at 26.19%

The mechanism is the one the builder's loop-gain guard was written for: the
shaped drive is notched at each resonance, the resonance slides off its own
notch into a hotter part of the drive spectrum, and that mode's local loop gain
passes 1 while the rest of the band is still well behaved.  The guard is
evaluated at the 0 dB design point only, so it does not see this.

Consequence for the run set: +4 dB and +8 dB are NOT "the same plant, driven
harder".  They are a plant with a few modes far past the design point and the
rest near it.  Run 49's +8 dB point should be read with that in mind, and it is
the likely mechanism behind the tail leakage into the linear control group seen
there.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULT 4 -- TWO DIFFERENT ANSWERS, AND BOTH ARE CORRECT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Everything above is the EQUIVALENT-LINEAR answer: the properties of the best
linear system matching the response in a mean-square sense.  That is the right
number for anything that consumes an FRF in an rms sense, which includes every
control law here.

A modal fit to a MEASURED FRF returns something different, because the
resonance wanders with the instantaneous amplitude across the record.  Nine
softened modes, direct time integration, white-band excitation (fig 9):

  - the FRF PEAK moves LESS than the equivalent-linear frequency, by a factor
    0.836 on average (range 0.676 - 0.962).  Small excursions dominate by count
    and they soften least, so the peak sits above the mean-square answer.

  - the HALF-POWER width reads HIGH, and the error grows with the shift:

        shift measured      apparent zeta / true zeta
           0.26 %                  1.004
           0.31 %                  1.012
           0.51 %                  1.030
           0.84 %                  1.078
           1.24 %                  1.178
           1.33 %                  1.220
           1.42 %                  1.334
           1.81 %                  1.599
           2.35 %                  1.678

    roughly 1 + 0.12*(shift in %)^2.  At the 0 dB design point the inflation is
    a few percent; by +4 dB a half-power fit would report damping 30-60% above
    the true equivalent-linear value with no damping mechanism having changed.

So "how much does the damping change" has two answers that diverge exactly
where the plant is most interesting.  Any damping number taken off a measured
FRF on this article above about 1% shift is an upper bound, not a measurement.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VERIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Direct time integration, fs = 20 kHz, 24 realisations x 20 s = 480 s of record
per mode, the shaped run-40 drive at 0 dB.  Linear and nonlinear simulations
share identical noise and integrator so the scheme's own frequency bias cancels.

  mode      sigma_q sim/pred     c_eq meas/pred     E|qd|^3 / sigma_v^3
                                                    (Gaussian = 1.5958)
  300.9 Hz      1.0007              1.0003               1.5993
  464.4 Hz      1.1467              1.0917               2.2830
  728.2 Hz      1.0071              1.0027               1.6118
  899.5 Hz      1.0098              1.0039               1.6024

Three of four modes confirm the response to within 1% and the equivalent damping
coefficient to within 0.4%, with the measured E[|qd|^3] landing on the Gaussian
value to three digits.  That is the Gaussian assumption tested directly rather
than assumed.

The 464.4 Hz mode is the exception and it is informative rather than an error:
its velocity is markedly non-Gaussian (2.283 against 1.596), the response runs
15% above prediction and the damping 9% above.  It is the same mode that breaks
away first at +2 dB.  Heavy-tailed response and early runaway are the same
phenomenon seen two ways.

The frequency check is separated from the response check deliberately.  An H1
estimate formed from the shaped control drive is starved of excitation exactly
at the peak, because match_trace notches the drive there, so the measured peak
frequency is biased by however deep that mode's notch is.  The frequency
numbers in result 4 therefore come from flat-band excitation with sigma_q
measured from the simulation and the closed form evaluated at that measured
sigma_q -- nothing is assumed about what response the drive produces.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS DOES NOT COVER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Measured f and zeta extracted from the saved run 49/50/55 spectra.  At 1 Hz
  resolution a 0.745% shift on a 400 Hz mode is 3 lines and a 12% damping change
  widens the half-power band by about one line.  Frequency would confirm at the
  top levels; damping would not resolve.  Not attempted.
- Cross-coupling between modes.  Every calculation here treats the modal
  equations as uncoupled, which is what the virtual hardware also does.
- The +4 dB down-sweep point.  Hysteresis is unresolved and is a separate
  question from the level curve above.
