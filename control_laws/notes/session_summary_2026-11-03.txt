2  NAMED PARAMETER SYNTAX: HOW IT WORKS, AND ONE HOLE CLOSED          ef08cd87
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Where the parameters are defined: one Param table per law, in that law's own file.
    _BASE_PARAMS   optimal_diagonal_control.py            15 entries
    _FAST_PARAMS   optimal_diagonal_control_fast.py       10 entries
    _OCTAVE_PARAMS octave_band_switching_control.py        5 entries
parameter_table() collects them up the class chain: fast law 25, octave law 20.  The Param
class, the parser (_parse_named) and the readers live in the base file only; the other two
files load it by path.  Each Param = name, default, converter, legacy position, one-line doc.

A string with a ':' anywhere is the named form (one name: value per line, or ';', '#'
comments); a string without one is the old comma form.  Named mistakes raise ValueError with
the nearest valid name (Rattlesnake shows it in a dialog).  Legacy mistakes warn and keep the
default.

Tried GUI/spreadsheet-style inputs through the real constructors (the GUI passes
toPlainText() verbatim, a spreadsheet passes the cell value):
    fine     CRLF and CR line ends, tabs, non-breaking space, ';' separators, comments,
             capitals, empty values, a number-typed cell (still means field 1)
    loud     misspelled names, spaces/hyphens in a name, units or '%' in a value, smart
             quotes, two items on one line, a byte-order mark
    HOLE     a named line with its colon missing ("reg = 1e-5", "max_bins_per_update 10")
             was read as the comma form: one console warning, every default kept, the test
             ran on defaults.
FIX: in the comma branch, a field that holds a parameter name, an underscore word, '=' or a
full-width colon/semicolon now raises, asking for "name: value".  Real legacy strings
(numbers, db/linear) are untouched.
Checks: check_named_params.py 23/23 (two new tests; deleting the guard in a copy makes one
fail), golden_params 246/246 pairs and 112/112 named twins, golden_optdiag --strict 43/43.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Plant: frame_plant('10x14'): 10 drives, 14 controls, 100-1000 Hz, 5 Hz lines, flat spec.
15 closed-loop cycles, 20-frame CPSD estimates, FRF held fixed, defaults for every law
(octave law switched into octave mode with set_test_level_db(0); nothing in Rattlesnake
calls that hook yet).  band_average run with df 5, oct_order 6, drive_rms_limit none (the
plant's drive units are arbitrary).

Three different "octave" things; the file names say which:
    OCTAVE law            octave_band_switching_control.py.  A subclass of the SDP law; one
                          SDP per 1/6-octave band; averages H over the band (mean of H).
    BAND-AVERAGE law      band_average.py.  A separate law; minimum-norm drive per band from
                          the exact band cross-power; automatic regularization (kappa).
    octave_exactC         EXPERIMENT, not a repo law (tests/octave_exact_c.py): the octave
                          law with the exact band cross-power in place of the mean of H.

rms dB error vs the spec.  First number: the 11 channels the SDP/fast laws can reach.

4  WHY THE OCTAVE-BASED LAWS MISS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(a) OCTAVE LAW: the mean-of-H band average is the error.  With the exact band cross-power
    C = mean over the band's lines of (h_m h_m^H) in place of the mean of H, the band error
    on the 11 reachable channels falls from 2.21 to 0.32 dB (all 14: 3.20 -> 2.50, better
    than the SDP law's 2.61).  Per line it only improves 3.22 -> 2.66 dB, because a drive
    that is flat inside a band cannot follow resonances.  C needs only the per-line H the law
    already receives, so the "known limitation" in its docstring does not apply.
(b) OCTAVE LAW: the top band is diluted.  Its last band (945-1060 Hz) takes in lines above
    fmax where the spec is zero, so its target is the band mean including zeros, about half.
    From the code (_oct_y_target is a mean over all the band's lines); not tested alone.
(c) BAND-AVERAGE LAW: looked worst (4.57 dB rms, all 14, in its own bands).  Cause, found
    with tests/bandavg_gap.py: its automatic condition number is too low.
      - The law's knee sweep (_resolve_auto_kappa) predicts each kappa with the plain
        regularized fit and drops negative eigenvalues.  The real solve (_solve_bands ->
        _realizable) refits over PSD drives when that costs more than 0.1 dB.
      - So the sweep's predicted error RISES with kappa (2.5 dB at 18 -> 11.5 at 1e5, mean
        |dB|) while the real solve's error FALLS (2.06 at 18 -> 0.9 from kappa 100 up).
      - The sweep picks kappa 18.  The same 0.1 dB rule on the real solve picks 100.
      - The earlier "predicts 2.5, achieves 4.6" was partly a metric difference: the sweep
        predicts mean |dB|, I scored rms dB.  The achieved mean |dB| is 2.06, so the law does
        meet its own forecast.
    Law's own bands, 20-frame closed loop (rms dB, all 14 / 11 reachable; drive level):
      auto kappa 18      4.57 / 3.36   -26.5 dB
      kappa 100          2.81 / 0.94   -21.6 dB
      kappa 1000         2.76 / 0.51   -19.9 dB (first solve)
      SDP law            2.92 / 0.11   about -12 dB
    So at kappa 100 the law is about as good as the SDP law on all 14, still far behind on
    the 11 reachable, and uses much less drive.
